# Geophysics Laboratory at CUHK

> Earthquake, volcanic, and Earth surface processes  
> Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences, The Chinese University of Hong Kong

The **Geophysics Laboratory at CUHK** studies earthquake, volcanic, and Earth surface processes, with the goal of improving hazard mitigation and supporting sustainable development.

We are based in the Department of Earth and Environmental Sciences at **The Chinese University of Hong Kong (CUHK)**, in the Mong Man Wai Building on the main campus.

## Our mission

Our mission is to better understand the physical processes that drive earthquakes, volcanic activity, and Earth surface hazards—while translating those insights into knowledge that can contribute to safer and more sustainable societies.

We are particularly known for applying machine learning to create high-resolution earthquake catalogues. Combined with advanced statistical analysis, these catalogues help reveal fault-system structures and volcanic plumbing processes that may otherwise remain hidden.

## Research themes

Our work spans five connected research themes:

### 1. Machine learning applications in geophysics

We develop and apply deep-learning phase pickers and classifiers to transform noisy, continuous seismic recordings into dense earthquake catalogues capable of resolving fine-scale fault structures.

### 2. Fault response to stress changes

We investigate how small, accurately measured stress perturbations influence fault slip behavior and what these responses reveal about fault friction.

### 3. Dynamics of volcanic systems

Our volcano seismology research covers submarine and subaerial environments, including microearthquakes, tremor, and seismo-acoustic signals associated with magma movement.

### 4. Low-frequency and long-period earthquakes

We study the clustering and scaling behavior of low-frequency earthquakes (LFEs) and long-period events as indicators of slow slip, fluid migration, and magma movement at depth.

### 5. Earth surface processes and hazard cascades

We use seismic records to investigate landslides, dam breaches, outburst floods, and the earthquakes associated with these cascading hazards.


## Fieldwork and data collection

Our research draws on observations from diverse environments, including:

- Active volcano flanks and crater lakes
- Fumarole fields
- Ocean ridges and research vessels
- Ocean-bottom seismometer deployments
- Deep-submergence vehicle expeditions
- Snow-covered volcanic terrain
- Shipboard seismic monitoring campaigns
